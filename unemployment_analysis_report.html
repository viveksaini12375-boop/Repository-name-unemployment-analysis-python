Unemployment Analysis: India, 2019–2020

Executive Summary

  • Covid-19 coincides with a sharp break in the observed unemployment series. The mean unemployment rate across available state-area records rose from 9.6% before April 2020 to 24.3% in April-May 2020, a 14.6 percentage-point increase.
  • The shock partly reversed by June. The June mean was 11.9%, substantially below the April-May level.
  • Urban and rural observations were both affected. The pre-Covid mean was 10.9% for urban records and 8.2% for rural records; both increased sharply in April-May.
  • Seasonality cannot be established confidently. The source contains only about one partial annual cycle and incomplete state-area coverage.

Monthly unemployment shows a pronounced April-May 2020 shock

The series is relatively stable through 2019 and early 2020, then jumps in April and May before falling in June. This is consistent with a major disruption around the pandemic period in the source, but the dataset alone does not prove causality.

Urban observations started higher, but both areas were hit

Urban observations have a higher pre-Covid mean than rural observations in this file. Both groups rise strongly in April-May, and by June their observed means are close to one another.

Seasonal patterns are suggestive, not conclusive

Pre-Covid monthly means vary, but there is not enough repeated yearly history to distinguish recurring seasonality from normal month-to-month variation. More years of monthly data are needed.

Recommended next steps

  • Treat April-May 2020 as the primary Covid shock window, not as ordinary seasonal variation.
  • Analyze state and urban/rural differences when considering employment support, because the shock is heterogeneous.
  • Add several years of monthly observations before performing formal seasonal decomposition.
  • Use labour-force/population weights for national estimates if the goal is an official aggregate rather than an equal-weight mean of records.
  • Review missing months and extreme values before formal modelling.

Caveats and assumptions

  • The source is a flattened pasted table, so records were reconstructed from its repeated structure.
  • Coverage is May 2019–June 2020, with missing state-area months in some groups.
  • Headline averages are equal-weight averages of available state-area records, not population-weighted national rates.
  • The analysis is descriptive; it does not isolate Covid from other contemporaneous economic effects.
